A child's first trip to the dentist sets the tone for years to come, so we like to make it **short, fun and completely pressure-free**.

## When should the first visit be?

We recommend bringing your child **when their first tooth appears, or by their first birthday**, whichever comes first. Early visits are mostly about getting comfortable, and about giving you, the parent, tips for those tiny teeth.

## What happens at the visit

- **Hello and a chat.** We ask about brushing, bottles or feeding, dummies and any family dental history.
- **A ride in the big chair.** Many little ones sit on a parent's lap, which is perfectly fine.
- **Counting teeth.** We take a gentle look at teeth and gums, often with just a little mirror.
- **Tips for home.** How much toothpaste to use, how to brush, and snack ideas that are kind to teeth.
- **A big well done!** Every visit ends with lots of praise (and usually a sticker).

Most first visits take **10–15 minutes**. If your child isn't in the mood, that's okay: we'll just say hello and try again another day.

## How to prepare your child

1. **Talk about it positively.** "The dentist will count your teeth and check they're strong!"
2. **Avoid scary words** like *hurt*, *needle* or *drill*, even to reassure ("it won't hurt" plants the idea).
3. **Read a picture book** or play "dentist" with a teddy at home.
4. **Pick a good time**: after a nap and a snack, not right before bedtime.
5. **Bring an older sibling's check-up along** so they can watch first.

## Looking after tiny teeth at home

- Start brushing **as soon as the first tooth appears**, twice a day.
- Under 3s: a **smear** of fluoride toothpaste (1,000 ppm). From 3 to 6: a **pea-sized** amount.
- Help with brushing until about **age 7**, when they can tie their own shoelaces.
- Keep sugary snacks and drinks to **mealtimes**, and offer water or milk in between.

We love meeting new little patients. If you have any questions before the visit, just give us a call.
