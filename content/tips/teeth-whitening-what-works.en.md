Everyone loves a bright smile, and there's no shortage of products promising one. Here's our honest, no-hype guide.

## Why teeth change colour

- **Surface stains** from coffee, tea, red wine, curry and smoking sit on the outside of the enamel.
- **Deeper discolouration** happens naturally with age, as enamel thins and the yellower layer underneath (dentine) shows through. Some medicines and old injuries can also darken a tooth.

Knowing which you have makes a big difference to what will work.

## What actually works

**Professional cleaning.** A scale and polish removes surface stains and tartar. Sometimes that's all you need, and it's the right first step anyway.

**Dentist-supervised whitening.** Gels containing hydrogen peroxide (or carbamide peroxide) gently lighten the tooth's natural colour from within. Options include:

- **Take-home trays**: custom-made trays you wear with a gentle gel for a set time each day over a couple of weeks.
- **In-clinic whitening**: a stronger treatment done in the chair, sometimes combined with take-home trays.

In the EU, products with more than 0.1% hydrogen peroxide may only be used after a dental examination and under a dentist's supervision, and not for under-18s. That's for your safety, not red tape.

## What has limited effect

**Whitening toothpastes** contain mild abrasives or polishing agents that help remove *surface* stains. They won't change the natural shade of your teeth, but they can help keep results looking fresh.

**Over-the-counter strips** sold in the EU are limited to very low peroxide levels, so results are modest.

## What to skip

- **Charcoal powders and toothpastes**: there's little evidence they whiten, and they can be abrasive and wear enamel.
- **Lemon juice, vinegar or baking-soda scrubs**: acids and harsh scrubbing damage enamel, which can make teeth look *more* yellow over time.
- **Unregulated online kits**, especially those with no stated ingredients or strength.

## Good to know before you whiten

- Whitening only works on **natural teeth**. Fillings, crowns and veneers keep their colour, so they may need replacing to match.
- **Temporary sensitivity** is common and usually fades within a few days.
- Results last longer if you go easy on staining foods and drinks and keep up regular cleans.
